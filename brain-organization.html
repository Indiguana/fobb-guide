Organizing & dissecting the brain

The six cortical layers, the directions and planes anatomists use, the four ways to carve up the brain, and what you saw in the sheep-brain dissection.

1. Cortical columns and the six layers

The cerebral cortex is the thin, folded outer layer of the cerebrum. Cut straight down through it and you see six distinct layers, stacked like floors in a building, organized into columns. Each floor has a different job in the building's mail system:

Who talks to whom in a cortical column — click a layer group
What to notice: input and output live on different floors. The thalamus is both where most input comes from and where layer 6 sends output back.
Common mistake — the source changed The Lecture 2 study guide says "Layer 1 = cortex-to-cortex, Layer 6 = output". Lecture 3 opened by correcting this after exit tickets showed confusion: Layers 1, 2, 3 = connections from one cortical region to another; Layers 5, 6 = output (to thalamus); Layer 4 = input (from thalamus and other cortex). Use the Lecture 3 version. You'll use "LGN → layer 4 of V1" again in Chapter 10.

Slice the brain — pick a plane
You look at a slice showing both hemispheres side by side, with the top of the head at the top and both temporal lobes hanging down on either side. Which plane is it?
Why: A slice that shows both hemispheres and runs top-to-bottom goes through both ears — coronal. A sagittal slice shows only one hemisphere (front-to-back). A horizontal slice would show both hemispheres but viewed from above, front at one end and back at the other.

3. Four ways to divide the brain

Why four? Because "where does one part end and the next begin" has different answers depending on what you measure — tissue colour, cell shape, folds, or function.

Schematic, not to scale. What to notice: the lobes are defined by landmarks (the big sulci), which is exactly why the lecture says sulci and gyri are used as landmarks for surgery.
DivisionBased onDetails from lecture
1. Grey vs white matterTissue colourGrey = cell bodies (somas). White = axons — white because of the fatty myelin.
2. CytoarchitecturalCell compositionBrodmann areas: divisions based on the size, shape and density of neurons in different regions of cortex.
3. AnatomicalFoldsFour lobes: frontal, parietal, occipital, temporal. Gyri (ridges) and sulci (grooves) form to maximize surface area; used as surgical landmarks.
4. FunctionalWhat a region doesSpecific locations have specific functions — but general functions like language are distributed across regions. Historical (wrong) version: phrenology — skull bumps linked to personality (chart from 1883; idea goes back hundreds of years).
Across species Compared with other animals, humans have more grey matter (neurons), more folds (convolutions), and more white-matter pathways.
Across people Human brains differ in size and shape, but general organization is consistent. Because we often study group-average brains, we can miss small indentations (tertiary sulci). Manually labelling 4,511 sulci in 572 hemispheres found a new one — the inframarginal sulcus, absent from atlases.
Intuition Why fold at all? A sheet of cortex needs area (more neurons), but the skull is a fixed box. Crumpling a sheet lets a large area fit in a small volume — like crumpling paper into a ball. More folds = more cortex per skull.

5. Check yourself

White matter looks white because it is mostly…
Why: Myelin is fatty and pale. Cell bodies are the grey matter.
A map dividing cortex by the size, shape and density of its neurons is a…
Why: "Cyto" = cell; cytoarchitecture = cellular composition. Lobes are defined by folds; functional maps by what regions do; phrenology by skull bumps.
A tumour sits at the caudal end of the brain. It's most likely in the…
Why: Caudal = back ("tail"). The occipital lobe is the back of the cerebrum. Frontal would be rostral; top of parietal would be dorsal.
Axons arriving from the thalamus mostly terminate in cortical layer…
Why: Layer 4 is the input layer. Layer 6 is where output goes back to the thalamus — the tempting mix-up.
